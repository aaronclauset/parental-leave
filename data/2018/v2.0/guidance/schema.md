# Capture schema

This is the field reference for the structured records of faculty parental-leave policies: one record per institution, with one component per provision. It is version 1.25, the version under which the 2018 data set v2.0 was produced. It is read with the interpretive rules (`rules.md`), the coding checklist (`coding_checklist.md`), the field meanings (`field_meanings.md`) and the reader instructions (`reader_instructions.md`), which share its version number. Interpretive rules are in `rules.md` and are not repeated here.

Throughout, **v1.0** means the earlier data set: the faculty parental-leave data published with Morgan, Way, Galesic, Larremore and Clauset (2018), with its corrections through January 2019. This release (**v2.0**) re-collects the same institutions from the policies in force in the 2017–18 academic year.

The same guidance governs later collections (a 2026 collection is under way); the examples are from the 2018 data. Published with the data: the records, this guidance, `programs.csv`, `statutory_baseline.csv`, the 2017–18 calendar table (`calendar_2017_18.csv`), the code and the provenance index. Not published: the source documents and saved statute texts (identified by URL and web-archive capture in the provenance index), the passage index, the readers' reports, and the checker the readers ran (`check_extraction.py`), which tests a record against the saved source texts.

**This is the single field reference.** The reader instructions, the checker used during collection (`check_extraction.py`, not published) and the derivation code (`derive.py`) all follow it. A field not listed here is not read by any code; put anything else in `description` or a `*_note` field.

**The guiding rule:** record what the policy says, in its own terms. Summary figures (the 2018-definition figure, totals, unpaid weeks) are derived afterwards by code (§3) and are never typed in by hand.

**Format:** one JSON record per institution, `<slug>.json`. Coded fields take exactly the codes listed; free-text fields take the policy's words. Omit a field you cannot fill rather than guessing, except where a field is marked **required**.

## 1. Institution record

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| `institution`, `slug` | **Required.** Name as in v1.0 or the sampling frame, that is, the 205 U.S. and Canadian universities in v1.0 (v1.0's second row for the Polytechnic Institute of NYU, which repeated NYU's policy, is not kept); the record's short identifier |
| `country` | **Required.** `US` or `Canada` |
| `is_private`, `census_region`, `prestige_rank_wapman` | Institution metadata (`is_private`, `census_region`, `prestige_rank_wapman`, AAU membership) are columns of the data set CSV, not record fields; `prestige_rank_wapman` is from Wapman et al. (2022). |
| `reference_date` | The date on which the documents are read as policy in force |
| `calendar` | `{"term_weeks": <number>, "kind": "semester" \| "quarter" \| "term", "note": "<evidence>"}`. `term_weeks` holds only a length a document states for the institution's term, semester or quarter, in weeks, with the quote in `note`. Where a document states the kind of term but not its length, `term_weeks` is left empty and the kind is recorded in `kind`. Omit the object when no document states either. The derivation takes a term's length in this order (rule R8): the stated length; the institution's academic calendar for the reference year (`calendar_2017_18.csv` for the 2018 data); v1.0's term length for the institution; the default for the kind |
| `leave_limits` | A list, one entry per stated maximum on the **time** a parent may spend on leave for one event, paid or unpaid, across several components: `{"weeks": <number>, "components": ["<id>", …], "roles": ["birth" \| "nonbirth"], "faculty_group": "<group>", "waivable_at_discretion": true, "note": "<the wording>", "evidence": ["verbatim quote"], "decision_log": [<entries as for a component>]}`. Each entry carries its own `decision_log`: at least one entry with `field` `leave_limits`, giving the conversion of `weeks` and the choice of components and roles. `weeks` is converted from the stated unit by the record's calendar (one year = 52; a quarter or semester as in rule R8). `faculty_group` is optional: the group the limit is stated for, as for a component (§2b). `waivable_at_discretion` is optional: `true` where the institution may waive the limit at its discretion; the limit then binds the guaranteed figures only (rule R20). A limit stated over a window longer than one event ("12 months in any three years") is recorded at its value for one event, with the window in `note`. An allowance stated per window ('16 weeks in year one and 12 in year two in any two-year window'; 'N weeks per year') is coded as one component at its amount for the first window, with the later windows' amounts in `duration_note`; the values never join allowances of successive windows into one continuous leave. Where another leave's time in the same window reduces it, say so in `duration_note`; as an exception to this row's first sentence, record no entry that would also cap the other leave (Yale 2018 Caregiver's Leave → 16 weeks). Relief counts only where the text includes it. Not recorded here: limits on paid weeks across all sources (`cap_weeks_all_sources`), caps on the paid weeks of named benefits (`paid_caps`), tenure-clock totals, and totals already expressed as a relief's length with `within`. The derivation places no more than `weeks` of the listed components, in protected weeks and in the paid figures (rule R20) |
| `paid_caps` | A list, one entry per stated cap on the **paid** weeks of two or more named benefits together (not all paid sources, which is `cap_weeks_all_sources`): `{"weeks": <number>, "components": ["<id>", …], "roles": ["birth" \| "nonbirth"], "note": "<the wording>", "evidence": ["verbatim quote"]}`. `roles` is optional (both roles when absent). It limits only the paid figures for the listed components (the 2018-definition figure, full-pay-equivalent weeks and the paid total); it does not limit leave time (`leave_limits`) or protected weeks (rule R20) |
| `statutory_floor` | `{"jurisdiction", "year", "weeks_birth", "weeks_nonbirth", "statute", "source_doc", "table"}`: the statutory leave floor of the record's jurisdiction (rule U7). Filled by code (`statutory_floor.py`) from the floor columns of `statutory_baseline.csv` for the institution's jurisdiction and the record's year; never filled by a reader. Absent, with a note in `notes`, where the table has no row for the year |
| `documents` | List of `{doc_id, url, title, version_or_effective_date, retrieved}` for the documents read, with `wayback_timestamp` where the copy is a web-archive capture. `version_or_effective_date` is empty when the document states no version or date. The documents themselves are not published; `url` and the capture identify them |
| `missing` | `false`, or a reason: `login_required`, `not_found`, `scan_only` or `no_policy_on_page`. No other value is used |
| `components` | List (§2) |
| `notes` | Free text, a list of strings: the governing documents, the reading of the policy, conflicts between sources, and documents cited but not saved. The record of which files and lines were read belongs in the reader's report, not here |

A reader's output adds `model`, `codebook_version`, `schema_version`, `scope_accounting`, `passage_dispositions`, `mechanism_slots` and `baseline_accounting` (formats in the reader instructions). `codebook_version` and `schema_version` each hold the guidance version the record was read under, as a plain number such as `"1.25"`: no prefix or file name.

| Field | Format |
|---|---|
| `scope_accounting` | **Required.** One entry per scope class in the coding checklist §1 (`C1`–`C10`): `{"components": ["<id>", …]}`, or `{"absent": {"documents": ["<doc_id>", …], "terms": ["…"]}}`. An entry has one or the other, not both. Every class must be present, and every component appears under at least one class. Under C4, a statute named in the documents but not a component (U4) is listed as `{"not_components": [{"statute": "…", "reason": "…"}]}` beside the class's components or absence |

## 2. Component record (one per provision)

A **component** is one provision that gives a new parent time off, pay, relief from duties, or protection (job protection or tenure-clock protection). One provision, one component (rule V4): a bundle is split and the parts are linked with `sequence`.

A **long-term disability plan** (one the documents call long-term disability, or whose function is income replacement after short-term disability or sick pay ends) is recorded as one component, `generic_sick_or_disability_plan`, with `faculty_group`, `eligible_parent` (`["birth_parent"]`), `entitlement`, `payer`, evidence and decision log, and no `duration`, since no childbirth length can come from it. A stated waiting (elimination) period goes in `time_window`, and stated eligibility in `service_requirement`. Optional buy-up coverage the employee pays for is `employee_paid` (R1d). Short-term disability, salary continuation and staged short-term plans are coded in full.

### 2a. Identity and type

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| `component_id` | **Required.** `<slug>_cN`, N numbering the record's components |
| `institution_label` | The policy's own name for the provision, verbatim (e.g. "Active service-modified duties", "Paid Parental Leave"). **Empty string** when the provision has no name of its own (only a heading that covers several provisions); then `description` says what it is |
| `description` | One or two sentences in your own words: what the provision gives, to whom. Not evidence |
| `mechanism` | **Required.** One of: `employer_paid_leave` · `employer_childbirth_benefit` (a dedicated maternity, childbearing or birth benefit; rule V1) · `generic_sick_or_disability_plan` (childbirth handled like any other illness or disability) · `own_accrual` (the employee's own sick, vacation or compensatory balances) · `donated_leave` · `government_benefit` (EI, QPIP, a US state program, a federal program; rule M1) · `employer_topup` (pay added on top of a government benefit) · `modified_duties` (on active service, duties reduced) · `unpaid_leave` (FMLA, CFRA, ESA, institutional) · `tenure_clock` · `part_time_option` · `other` |
| `mechanism_note` | Why this mechanism, where it isn't obvious (e.g. the V1 three-part test) |
| `rule_applied` | List of rule ids used for this component, e.g. `["R9", "V1"]` |

### 2b. Who

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| `faculty_group` | **Required.** The code **`main_campus_tenure_stream`** whenever the covered group **includes** main-campus tenured and tenure-track faculty, whatever the policy calls it ("all employees", "academic staff", "faculty and librarians", "regular faculty", "benefits-eligible employees", "members of the bargaining unit" where that unit holds the professoriate). Otherwise the **name of the group**, in the policy's words, e.g. `"staff (non-faculty)"`, `"Health Sciences Compensation Plan"`, `"12-month A&P faculty"`, `"federal civilian employees"`. A group defined by appointment length that leaves out academic-year faculty (the institution's standard teaching appointment), such as '12-month faculty' or 'fiscal-year appointees', is recorded by name, not as `main_campus_tenure_stream`, even if some tenure-stream faculty hold such appointments. The derivation counts a component only if this field is the code |
| `faculty_group_note` | The policy's own words for the covered group, verbatim or close, whenever the code is used (so the inclusion can be checked) |
| `faculty_scope` | A restriction within the main campus, e.g. `"SAS and Hajim only"` (rule V3: arts-and-sciences plus engineering still counts as main campus) |
| `eligible_parent` | **Required.** List, any of: `birth_parent` · `non_birth_parent` · `adoptive` · `foster` · `surrogacy` (an intended parent through surrogacy; a surrogate who gives birth is `birth_parent`; rule R5) · `any_parent`. Use `any_parent` only when the text is gender- and role-neutral; otherwise list the roles it names |
| `eligible_parent_wording` | The gendered or role words used, verbatim ("mother", "father", "spouse or partner", "birth mother") |
| `caregiver_condition` | `none` · `primary` · `majority_or_50pct` · `equal_or_more_than_partner` · `other`; the definition in `caregiver_condition_wording` |
| `caregiver_condition_wording` | The policy's definition of the condition, verbatim. Expected whenever `caregiver_condition` is not `none` (the checker warns if it is missing) |
| `service_requirement` | As written: `"12 months and 1,250 hours"`, `"one semester"`, `"none stated"`. A component that requires more than two years of service carries the flag `service_over_two_years` (rule U8) |
| `dual_employee_rule` | As written: `"combined 12 weeks"`, `"each may take"` |

### 2c. Entitlement and conditions

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| `entitlement` | **Required** on every component (`tenure_clock` and `other` included). `guaranteed` (stated as an entitlement: "shall", "is entitled", "will receive", "receives", "has the right") · `guaranteed_on_request` (the text gives the benefit and approval covers only the arrangements: "may elect", "has the option", "is allowed", "is eligible for", "upon request"; rule V2) · `discretionary` (the approval decides whether there is a benefit: "may be granted", "at the discretion of", "may request" with no grant language) · `negotiated` (the benefit's amount or form is set by agreement with the chair or dean, with no guaranteed minimum; counted only in the figures that include discretionary provisions; where only the arrangements are agreed, `guaranteed_on_request`; rule R12). A benefit the institution must grant once it is requested or applied for ("shall be granted upon request", "applications shall be approved if the applicant is eligible") is `guaranteed_on_request`. A statutory entitlement (FMLA, CFRA, EI) stays `guaranteed` even where the statute requires notice or an application. Flag `ambiguous` where V2 does not settle it |
| `entitlement_note` | The reasoning, when V2 was needed |
| `must_exhaust_first` | List: `sick`, `vacation`, `compensatory`, … |
| `medical_certification` | `true` / `false`, with what is certified in `medical_certification_note` |
| `medical_certification_note` | What is certified, as written |
| `runs_concurrently_with` | List of statute names or component ids that run at the same time (e.g. `["FMLA"]`); for a link between two components prefer `sequence` |
| `sequence` | A list of `{"relation": <code>, "component_id": "<id in this record>"}`. Each entry says how *this* component (the one carrying the entry) sits in time relative to the other. Each relation says one thing: `consecutive_after`: this one starts when the other ends · `within`: this one lies inside the other: it starts when the other starts (or `start_offset_weeks` later) and ends no later · `concurrent_with`: the two start together (or this one `start_offset_weeks` later) and overlap; neither need contain the other; statutory leave running with paid leave (rule S1) · `also_available`: a parent may take both, and neither replaces the other; the documents do not fix their order or overlap; the figures place them one after another (the longest-leave convention), within any `leave_limits` · `in_lieu_of`: alternatives; a parent takes one or the other (an option of several components carries the link on its leave and on each paid component: R15b). How to choose: use the most specific relation the text supports. Where the text lets this component start when the other ends, or later, use `consecutive_after` (the longest-leave convention). Relief for the rest of the semester in which a leave falls, coded with the semester as its length, contains the leave: the leave is `within` the relief (rule R3b). Add `"note"` to an entry when useful. Optional `start_offset_weeks` (a number): with `concurrent_with` or `within`, the weeks after the other component's start at which this one starts; a period stated in days is converted to weeks by rule R11 or R11b ("7 calendar days" → 1). **Direction:** record a relation once, on the component it describes (the one that follows, lies within, or starts with an offset). A reverse entry is not needed; where one is present it must say the same thing, and an entry with `start_offset_weeks` wins over a reverse entry without one. `consecutive_after` links must not form a cycle (the checker reports a cycle as an error). **Links and roles:** a link counts for a role only when both components count for that role. Otherwise it is ignored for that role, and the component is placed by its other links or by the default order (rule R15). So a bonding leave `consecutive_after` a birth-only disability leave (S1a) follows it for the birth parent, and is placed without it for the non-birth parent. Split a component by role only where its terms differ by role (`duration_by_role`). Optional `roles` (a list of `birth`, `nonbirth`): the entry applies only to the listed roles; for other roles it is ignored, and the component is placed by its other entries or by the default order (R15). Use it where the text places a component differently by role and the component is otherwise the same for every role, for example a parental leave that starts after the birth parent's disability period but at once for the other parent. Where the terms themselves differ by role, use `duration_by_role` or split the component. |
| `time_window` | As written: `"within 12 months of birth"` |
| `use_limit`, `use_limit_note` | A list of stated limits on how many times this component may be used, or for how many children (rule U9). Each entry is `{"times": N, "per": "career" \| "window", "window_years": X (with "window" only), "counts": "uses" \| "children"}`; `counts` defaults to `"uses"`. The policy's wording goes in `use_limit_note`. "Once per career" → `{"times": 1, "per": "career"}`; "twice per career" → `{"times": 2, "per": "career"}`; "no more than once every three years" → `{"times": 1, "per": "window", "window_years": 3}`; "for a maximum of two … children" → `{"times": 2, "per": "career", "counts": "children"}`. Absent: no limit stated, or only a renewal with each birth (U9). A cap on total time off the tenure clock (rather than a count of stops) is not a `use_limit`: it goes in the clock component's `duration_note`, with a flag (U9). A judgment field (evidence and decision log); where it goes: rule U9 |
| `return_obligation` | As written: `"repay if resign within 1 year"` |
| `notice` | As written |
| `flags` | List of strings, each starting with a code: `ambiguous: <reason>` · `conflicting_sources: <doc ids>` (rule V3d: the reading not taken where governing documents disagree; or a summary that misdescribes a governing document, U9) · `cross_reference_not_saved: <title>` · `cross_reference_not_fetched: <title>` (set during document collection, not by the reader) · `statutory_detail_not_in_sources: <statute>` (a statute the documents name whose terms are in neither the documents nor the saved statute texts) · `audit: <note>` (a note recording a correction to the coding made after the reading) · `not_yet_effective: <date>` (rule V5; excluded from derived values) · `summary_only: <doc_id>` (a provision found only in a summary or FAQ, not in the governing document; source precedence, rules §5; excluded from derived values) · `general_purpose` (a leave, paid or unpaid, that parents may also use but that exists for any purpose; rule U1; counted in no figure) · `excludes_main_faculty` (the provision names main-campus faculty as excluded) · `version_uncertain` (a document whose version on the reference date is uncertain) · `statutory_detail_not_in_sources: <act>` (a statute is named but neither the documents nor the saved statute texts give the detail) · `conditional_refusal: <ground>` (rule V2b: granted by default, but the institution may refuse on a stated finding such as undue burden or best interests; coded `guaranteed_on_request` and kept identifiable as not a binding requirement) · `own_illness_leave` (a leave for the birth parent's own illness or disability; counted in protected weeks only up to 52 weeks after the birth; rule U5) · `family_illness_leave` (a leave to care for a seriously ill family member; for the non-birth parent, counted in protected weeks only up to 52 weeks after the birth and in no paid figure; rule U5) · `open_ended_medical_leave` (a leave with no stated end that is not only the employee's own illness: a pregnancy or childbirth disability leave, or a medical leave that serves both self and family care; counted in protected weeks to 52 weeks after the birth for each parent it serves; leaves that end before the birth are not flagged; rule U5) · `service_over_two_years` (the component requires more than two years of service; counted in no figure; rule U8) |

### 2d. How much

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| `duration` | `{"value": <number>, "units": <unit>, "qualifier": <qualifier>}`. **Units exactly as written**, one of: `week`, `workweek`, `day`, `workday`, `calendar_day` (text says "calendar days"; 7 per week, R11b), `hour`, `month`, `semester`, `quarter`, `term`, `year`, `course`. **Qualifier** one of: `exact` (the text fixes the length), `up_to` ("up to", "maximum", "not to exceed", and "normally not more than" per rule Q1), `at_least` (rule R10), `normally` ("normally six weeks" with no maximum stated). A range or either-or ("six to eight weeks", "six or eight weeks") is coded as its lower value with qualifier `at_least`, the upper value in `duration_max` and the wording in `duration_note` (rule R13b) |
| `duration_max` | Same format as `duration` (`{value, units}`): where the text states a range or either-or, the upper value (rule R13b), or, for a benefit that grows with service, the band beyond the two-year member's (U8). The derivation reports `weeks_2018def_max` with it |
| `duration_note` | Free text: the range, the "normally", anything not captured |
| `job_protected_weeks` | A number of weeks: where the text guarantees return to the job (or reinstatement) for only part of the leave, the weeks so guaranteed; `0` where the text disclaims reemployment. Absent where the job is protected for the whole leave. The standard narrow exceptions (FMLA key employees, a position eliminated, a statute's own exceptions) do not limit it. Protected weeks use the shorter of this and the leave; the record keeps the stated length in `duration` (rule U3) |
| `paid_duration` | Same format as `duration`. Only when one provision pays part of its own length (a 12-week leave of which the first 6 are paid). Where the pay comes from a different mechanism than the leave (a top-up, a government benefit, a sick-leave plan), they are separate components linked by `sequence` (rule V4). The derivation uses it, when present, instead of `duration` for the paid figures |
| `duration_by_role` | `{"birth": {duration}, "nonbirth": {duration}}` when the length depends on the parent's role in the same provision (e.g. birth mother 2 terms, others 1 term). The derivation uses the role's entry, when present |
| `duration_variants` | Free text or a small object naming the conditions behind the lengths (`{"vaginal": "6 weeks", "c_section": "8 weeks"}`). Not read by code. A length that depends on a stated condition is coded as a range (rule R13b): the shorter in `duration`, the longer in `duration_max` |
| `pay_fraction` | As written: `"100%"`, `"95% integrated with EI"`, `"one-half salary"` |
| `pay_fraction_pct` | **A number 0–100:** the pay the employee receives during the period, as a percentage of normal pay (gross, including any government benefit a top-up is integrated with). Filled by code from `pay_fraction` ("100%", "full pay", "with pay", "no reduction", "one-half", "95% integrated with EI" → 95); a reader fills it by hand only when the checker reports it could not be parsed. Omit when the pay level is not stated. **Staged pay** ("70% for 7 weeks, then 55% for 25") is recorded as stated in `pay_fraction`, and `pay_fraction_pct` is left empty: no averaging. Relief or leave with a pay cut smaller than the reduction in duties carries its pay level here and counts at that level (rule R3). Used by rule R15b (choosing among alternatives; the `fullpay_equiv_weeks` column) |
| `payer` | `employer` · `government` (EI, QPIP) · `state_program` · `insurance_plan` (a state program through a private carrier, rule M1; a disability insurer) · `own_balance` · `employee_paid` (insurance the employee buys; rule R1d; counted in no paid figure; a dedicated leave so funded still counts in protected weeks, U3) · `mixed` · `none` |
| `pay_cap` | As written |
| `cap_note` | The wording of the cap in `cap_weeks_all_sources`. Expected with it (the checker warns if it is missing) |
| `cap_weeks_all_sources` | **A number of weeks**: a cap on the **paid** weeks across all sources (e.g. "sick leave plus parental leave may not exceed 13 weeks" → `13`). A limit on leave time, paid or unpaid, goes in `leave_limits`. Put the wording in `cap_note`. A cap on the paid weeks of two or more named benefits only (not all paid sources) is not recorded here but in the record's `paid_caps`. The derivation applies, for each role and variant, the smallest cap stated by a component counted there, to the paid figures (the 2018-definition figure, full-pay-equivalent weeks and the paid total), not to protected weeks (rule R20) |

### 2e. Relief by duty (only for leave or modified duties)

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| `relief` | Object with the five keys `teaching`, `service_admin`, `research`, `advising_supervision`, `clinical`, each one of `relieved` · `partially_relieved` · `continues` · `not_stated` |
| `relief_inferred` | `"formal leave"` when a leave of absence's relief is not stated and every duty is coded `relieved` (rule R9); `"unnamed duties continue (R4b)"` when a relief provision names some duties and the rest are coded `continues` |
| `relief_note` | Free text (e.g. 50% relief, spread over two semesters, rule R3c) |

### 2f. Evidence

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| `evidence` | **Required.** Object `{"<field>": ["verbatim quote", …]}`. Quotes are checked verbatim (whitespace-normalized) against the institution's saved sources and the saved statute texts. **Required for the judgment fields:** `mechanism` (only when rule V1 or R2 decides it), `faculty_group`, `eligible_parent`, `entitlement`, `duration`, `paid_duration`, `duration_by_role`, `pay_fraction`, `relief` (when any duty is stated), `sequence`, `cap_weeks_all_sources`, `service_requirement`, `use_limit`, `caregiver_condition` (when not `none`). Optional for every other field. The quote for `duration` should contain the qualifier words; the quote for `entitlement` the entitlement words. Where the words are elsewhere in the same passage, add that sentence too |
| `decision_log` | **Required where a judgment field is filled by a rule or a reading.** A list of `{"field": "<field>", "rule": "<rule id>", "answer": "<the value coded>", "quote": "<the deciding quote, verbatim>"}`. One entry per judgment, not per field copied straight from the text; where several rules decide one field, one entry per rule. The judgment fields are those listed for `evidence`, plus `mechanism`, `payer`, the `general_purpose` decision (whether set or not; field name `general_purpose`), any `relief` value inferred under R4b or R9, and `sequence`. `rule` is a rule id, or `schema` (a direct schema classification) or `source_precedence`. Every key is non-empty, and the quote is checked verbatim like `evidence`. `field` is a component field, `general_purpose`, or (on a `leave_limits` entry) `leave_limits`. Where the field holds a single code (`mechanism`, `entitlement`, `payer`, `caregiver_condition`, `faculty_group`) or is `general_purpose` (`true`/`false`), `answer` equals the coded value; other answers are free text. The log lets every decision made under a rule be found (by `rule`), and lets a decision be checked against its quote without re-reading the sources |
| `doc_ids` | **Required.** The documents the quotes come from |
| `statute_source` | For statutory components: the act and section the length or eligibility was taken from, e.g. `"29 U.S.C. 2612(a)(1)"` (rule U2). An `unpaid_leave` with `statute_source` is a statutory leave (rules S1, R18). An agreement article that restates a statutory leave without adding length is coded as the statute; one that adds length is the institution's own leave, `concurrent_with` the statute. Pay the agreement adds during a statutory leave is pay `within` it (S1) |
| `statute_verified` | `true` once the value was checked against a saved statute text (among the saved statute texts or the institution's own sources); `false` if only the policy page was read |

## 3. Derived fields (computed by `derive.py`, never entered)

| Derived field | Rule |
|---|---|
| `leave_weeks_2018def_{birth,nonbirth}` | Employer-funded leave (`employer_paid_leave`, `employer_childbirth_benefit`, `employer_topup`), guaranteed only unless the discretionary variant is run (R12); own accruals and generic plans excluded (R1, R2); components placed in time and combined by R19, within any `leave_limits` and `paid_caps` |

The derivation writes each figure per parent (`_birth`, `_nonbirth`), once for the guaranteed variant and once with discretionary provisions (`--include-discretionary`). The data set CSV renames the columns for readers (for example `weeks_2018def_birth` is `birth_weeks`, and the discretionary variant ends in `_disc`); its data dictionary gives the full mapping.

Role: `birth` = the birth parent (v1.0 "woman"); `nonbirth` = the non-birth parent after a birth (v1.0 "man"); adoptive, foster and surrogacy provisions are recorded but not counted in either (R5). Caregiver conditions are ignored in the roles (R6). Excluded from every figure: components whose `faculty_group` is not the code; those flagged `excludes_main_faculty`, `not_yet_effective`, `summary_only`, `general_purpose` (U1) or `service_over_two_years` (U8); and those outside the role. Excluded from the **paid** figures only: pay with `payer: own_balance` (R1, R1b) or `employee_paid` (R1d), and leaves flagged `family_illness_leave` (U5); a leave so funded still counts in protected weeks (U3). Derived values are rounded to one decimal, half up.

### How codes enter the figures

- `eligible_parent`: `any_parent`, or empty: the component counts for both parents.
- `payer`: `own_balance` and `employee_paid` are not employer-funded and count in no paid figure. Every other payer value, `mixed` included, counts as employer-funded.
- `mechanism`:
  - `employer_paid_leave`, `employer_childbirth_benefit` and `employer_topup` count in the 2018-definition leave figure, the full-pay-equivalent and paid totals, and protected weeks;
  - `modified_duties` counts as relief;
  - `government_benefit` counts in `paid_weeks_total` and in protected weeks' placement;
  - `generic_sick_or_disability_plan` counts only in `generic_plan_weeks_max`;
  - `unpaid_leave` counts only in protected weeks;
  - `own_accrual`, `donated_leave`, `part_time_option`, `tenure_clock` and `other` count in no figure.

  A pool that colleagues fund is coded `donated_leave` when the documents present it as its own program, and inside `own_accrual` (R1) when it is part of the employee's own balance rules.
- `relief`: a relief period whose teaching is `relieved` or `partially_relieved` and whose research `continues` is "Just teaching"; any other relief is "Other" (R4).
- **Units:**
  - `week` and `workweek` = 1 week;
  - `calendar_day` = 1/7;
  - `day` and `workday` = 1/5;
  - `hour` = 1/40;
  - `month` = 52/12;
  - `year` = 52;
  - `semester`, `quarter` and `term` = the term length (R8);
  - any other unit (`course`…) is not converted and counts in no figure, with a note.
- **Qualifiers** (`exact`, `up_to`, `at_least`, `normally`) are recorded but do not change the value used; a range's upper value is used only in the `_max` figures (R13b).
