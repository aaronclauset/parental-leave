The Faculty Parental Leave Project

The Faculty Parental Leave Project aims to measure, track, and understand the parental-leave benefits that universities provide to faculty. Parenthood is among the most impactful events in a faculty career, and that impact can vary broadly across individuals and across institutions. This project aims to measure that variability, identify patterns in leave benefits, and understand how parenthood is changing for faculty.

Parental leave policies are often complicated, and many faculty are not aware of the benefits their own university provides. The Faculty Parental Leave Project aims to systematically collect and analyze these policies, and to provide open data for researchers to use to better understand how parenthood impacts academic careers.

This page describes several highlights of the project so far, and links to the data sets and their documentation for researchers who want to use them.

Update, November 2026: a new version of the 2018 data (v2.0) is available. It re-collects the policies of the same universities from each institution's own policy documents, and adds measures of pay and job protection. What changed.

1Measuring parental leave benefits

Parental leave benefits typically come in two forms. A leave is time away from all duties, often paid. Relief (or modified duties) keeps the faculty member employed at full pay while removing some duties, usually teaching.

The primary value measures, for each parent, the number of weeks of employer-paid leave or of full-pay relief, whichever is longer. Relief that follows a paid leave adds to this value. Pay drawn only from the faculty member's own sick or vacation balances is counted as zero, as is a general disability plan that has no provision specific to childbirth.

We record each value in two ways. The guaranteed value counts benefits a faculty member is entitled to, including those granted on request. The value with discretionary benefits adds in those benefits the institution may grant or refuse. Values are recorded separately for the birth parent and for the non-birth parent (a birth parent's partner). Four secondary values describe pay and job protection more broadly:

Secondary values and their definitions
Secondary valueDefinition
Full-pay-equivalent weeksWeeks of employer-funded pay, each weighted by the share of salary paid
Paid weeks in totalWeeks with any pay because of the birth, from any source (employer, government benefit, insurance), excluding the faculty member's own balances
Sick-leave or disability plan weeksWeeks a birth parent could be paid through a general sick-leave or disability plan, at its short-term entitlement
Job-protected weeks (a maximum)The longest continuous period of job-protected leave because of the birth, including statutory leave; some of it requires a medical condition

Scope: main-campus tenured and tenure-track faculty with two years of service. Adoption and foster provisions are recorded in the policy encodings but are not counted here. The values describe entitlements granted by the policies.

    4Anatomy of a parental leave policy

    What a new parent receives as a benefit rarely comes from a single policy. It can depend on a parental-leave or childbirth policy, on sick leave and disability plans, on teaching relief, and even on statutes. To represent this complexity, each institution's policy was encoded as a set of components, one per provision that a document grants, linked in time. One component may run within, alongside, or after another component, or can be an alternative to it. The values are computed by placing the components on a timeline for each parent.       6Data collection protocol

      For v2.0, policy discovery, collection, and extraction were automated using a structured pipeline of large language models (Claude) and deterministic code. Models found each institution's documents, read them and encoded each policy. Deterministic code then checked every record and computed every value. Questions the coding rules did not settle were decided by the research team and written into the rules. This protocol is reusable, and produces detailed audit trails for data provenance and analysis.

      Find. Models search for each institution's documents in force in the 2017–18 academic year (faculty handbooks, collective agreements, leave policies and benefit summaries), preferring web-archive captures from that time, and save them with their provenance.
      Calendars. For institutions whose leave is given in terms, models find the 2017–18 academic calendar, from which code computes the length of a term in weeks.
      Read. One model reads every usable document of an institution in full and encodes the policy as a set of structured components, under a fixed coding guide. Each coded field is paired with a verbatim quote from the source documents that supports it.
      Check. Code verifies every quote against the saved documents, and every code, field and link in the encoding. Questions the guide did not settle were decided by the research team, written into the guide, and applied as recorded corrections.
      Compute. Code derives numerical values from the encodings. The released code rebuilds the data set from the released encodings exactly.

      An independent audit by a model from a different family re-read 20 randomly drawn institutions; 92.7% of its 600 values agreed with the data, and the disagreements were corrected. The whole collection used about 100 million tokens, or about $1,000 at API list prices as of November 2026.

      Limitations

      • A document's version in force on the reference date could not always be confirmed. Such components were flagged (n_version_uncertain in the data).
      • Policies were read by large language models. Each judgment was logged with its rule and quote, but some readings could be wrong.
      • For institutions the governing faculty policy was not publicly accessible, and no values were recorded.

      7Files, licenses and citation

      The files of the 2018 v2.0 release are in data/2018/v2.0/:

      • parental_leave_2018_v2.0.csv: the 2018 v2.0 data set, one row per institution; columns are described in the data dictionary
      • records/: the structured encoding of each institution's policy, with the verbatim quote supporting every coded field
      • guidance/: the coding rules, schema, checklist, field meanings, and reader instructions
      • provenance/index.csv: list of every source document, with its URL and web-archive capture
      • reference/: institution metadata, statutory baselines, programs, calendar term lengths and component tiers
      • code/: the code that rebuilds every data file from the encodings (see code/REPRODUCE.md)
      • v1_v2_differences.csv: v1.0 and v2.0 values side by side, with the cause of each difference
      • data/2018/v1.0/: archival copy of the 2018 v1.0 data set, and the 2018 website that presented it

      All files are in the GitHub repository aaronclauset/parental-leave. The data, encodings, guidance and reference tables are released under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License, and the code under the BSD 3-Clause license. The verbatim quotes in the encodings are short excerpts of documents whose copyright remains with their owners.
