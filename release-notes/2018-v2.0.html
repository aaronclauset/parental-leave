Release notes: the 2018 data, version 2.0

Version 2.0 of the 2018 data set, released in November 2026, re-collects the parental-leave policies of the same universities from each institution's own policy documents in force in the 2017–18 academic year, and adds measures of pay and job protection. These notes describe how v2.0 differs from v1.0, the version published in April 2018 and corrected through January 2019.

v1.0 had one more row, for the Polytechnic Institute of NYU, which repeated New York University's policy; it is not kept in v2.0. v1.0's values are not repeated in the v2.0 data set; they are compared here and in v1_v2_differences.csv.

1Agreement with v1.0

Where v2.0 and v1.0 differ, the v2.0 value is higher for of the birth-parent differences, lower for of , and different only in the kind of relief for of . In the remaining cases, no policy could be found now (). In v2.0, the median birth-parent primary value is weeks of paid leave or relief, compared to weeks in v1.0.

Comparing v1.0 against v2.0's source documents, each difference can be traced to a likely cause:

  • Rule differences. of the birth-parent differences follow from a rule that v2.0 applied and v1.0 did not, e.g., counting a semester of teaching relief that follows a paid leave in addition to it (as at Cornell), counting the weeks of a Canadian top-up, or the length of a term in the institution's own calendar.
  • Missing provisions. In , v2.0 identified a provision that v1.0 did not find, e.g., a teaching-relief policy in a faculty handbook or collective agreement where the v1.0 source was a human-resources page.
  • Errors. In , v1.0 contained errors, such as a leave reserved for birth mothers recorded for both parents, or Carleton's 12-week option recorded in place of its 51 weeks of top-ups.
  • Different reference dates. In , the parental leave policy changed between 1 January 2018 and the date that policy was measured in v1.0. Specifically, Virginia Tech's paid parental leave, which gave 8 weeks when it was coded in January 2019, was not yet in force on 1 January 2018. In addition, the University of Virginia's discretionary leave grew from 3 to 8 weeks, although that change only has an impact when we include discretionary benefits.

The cause of differences could not be determined, because no governing document could be obtained.

Institutions. Values for institutions could not be computed, because their governing faculty policies were not publicly accessible via the web: . v1.0 marked institutions as missing (). Reference dates. Source documents were read for the policies in force in the 2017–18 academic year. 4Rules that differ from v1.0

v2.0 keeps v1.0's primary value, but writes its definition down as rules (in guidance/rules.md) and applies them the same way everywhere. The rules below decide what the primary value counts where v1.0's practice was unstated or varied; identifiers are the guide's.

  • Relief after a leave (R3d, R3f). Relief that follows a paid leave adds to it, and linked relief periods form one period.
  • Pay from own balances (R1, R1b). Pay only from the faculty member's own sick, vacation or other balances counts zero weeks, even where the policy names a childbirth period.
  • General plans (R2, V1). A childbirth benefit counts; a general sick-leave or disability plan does not, unless the documents state a childbirth-specific period, its length and its pay.
  • Canadian top-ups (R7). In Canada, the weeks of an employer's top-up of EI count; the government benefit itself counts only in the paid total.
  • Term lengths (R8). A term is converted with, in order, a length the documents state, the institution's 2017–18 academic calendar (longest regular term, rounded up to whole weeks), v1.0's length, and 16 weeks for a semester or 11 for a quarter.
  • Ranges (R13b). A stated range counts at its lower value; the upper value is kept in the _max columns.
  • Discretionary benefits (R12, V2). Benefits the faculty member is given, with approval only of the arrangements, are guaranteed; benefits whose approval decides whether there is one are discretionary, and are counted only in the _disc columns.
  • The two parents (R5, R6). The two parents are the birth parent and their partner; adoption and foster provisions are recorded but not counted; primary-caregiver conditions are gender-neutral.
